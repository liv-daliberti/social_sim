\subsection{Experiment 2: Context-Conditioned Relationship Selection}
\label{sec:exp2}

Experiment~2 asks which numerical relationship applies. Reference cases define a
qualitative cue linking a new target to one of two response regimes; matched
correct- and opposite-context prompts and accumulating target observations test
selection and subsequent revision. Full prompts, analysis details, and per-arm
results appear in Appendix~\ref{app:coin-city}.

\textbf{Correct-context contrast.}
With no City C observations ($k=0$), adding its context lowers MAE relative to the
matched no-context arm for every deployment. Improvements range from $0.37$ to
$0.92$ poll points, with all bootstrap intervals excluding zero; the cross-model
median falls from 2.59 to 1.85. All numeric inputs are identical in the two arms.
Forecast-implied responses correlate $.52$--$.70$ with City C's true responsiveness,
compared with $-.06$ to $.08$ without context. By $k=4$, the MAE advantage narrows
to $0.12$--$0.23$ because City C's own cases reveal the relationship, which also
erases a $k=0$ advantage over a context-blind OLS benchmark
(Appendix Table~\ref{tab:coin-city-vs-ols}).

\textbf{Opposite-context contrast.}
At $k=0$, the opposite label raises MAE to $4.20$--$4.36$, $1.52$--$1.76$ points
above no context, and response correlations become $-.50$ to $-.68$. As City C
observations accumulate, the wrong-label penalty falls to $-0.01$--$+0.38$ points
at $k=4$. Models therefore use the cue when evidence is sparse and reduce its
influence when observations disagree.

\textbf{Models infer a new relationship from demonstrations and apply it to a
target.}
The arbitrary-label mapping reverses across episodes and must be inferred from
their examples. At $k=0$, Qwen3-14B and Qwen3-32B, all four Qwen2.5 checkpoints,
Llama~3.1-70B, and all five hosted systems recover it. Within Qwen3 the recovery
interval excludes zero only from 14B upward and within Llama only at 70B, but the
transition differs by family and is not monotone in parameter count. Kimi, both
Opus deployments, and GPT also lower forecast error, separating induction from
numerical calibration (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:coin-city-symbol-context}).

\textbf{Forecasts track the cue-named reference, not a lexical prior.}
The reference sentences state which regime responds more strongly, so the semantic
contrast alone cannot exclude a prior on those words. Partialling out the regime
separates them: at $k=0$ the implied slope tracks the cue-named reference's own
noisy fitted slope at $.51$--$.86$ and the other at $.04$--$.10$, both load equally
with no cue, and inversion moves the loading onto the reference the false cue names
(Appendix~\ref{app:coin-city-reference-selection}).

\textbf{Hidden states carry the induced relationship, weakly.}
Probes decode the cue-selected regime across six open checkpoints (AUC
$.844$--$1.000$), but that is largely lexical: input embeddings alone reach
$1.000$. Under arbitrary labels with 88 sealed episodes the embedding control falls
to $.555$ while held-out AUC stays at $.678$ ($p=.0016$); slope $R^2$ is $.016$ and
does not reject its null (Figure~\ref{fig:coin-city-mechanistic-main};
Appendix~\ref{app:coin-city-mechanistic-probe}).

The cue-reversal and evidence-accumulation contrasts test reference selection,
response application, and revision. Experiment~3 tests transfer after
episode-matched training.
