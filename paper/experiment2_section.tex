\subsection{Experiment 2: Context-Conditioned Relationship Selection}
\label{sec:exp2}

Experiment~2 asks which numerical relationship a model applies to a new target.
Two reference cities demonstrate a strong and a weak response regime, a
one-sentence cue assigns target City C to one of them, and City C's own cases
accumulate from $k=0$ to $k=4$. Arms are matched on every displayed number, so a
contrast isolates the cue. Those sentences state which regime responds more
strongly, so the semantic arms measure how a stated assignment becomes a number;
the induction claim rests on an arbitrary-label arm whose mapping reverses across
episodes. Appendix~\ref{app:coin-city} gives prompts and analysis details.

\textbf{Selection and revision.} The cue lowers zero-shot error for every
deployment, by $0.37$ to $0.92$ poll points with all six paired intervals
excluding zero, and every deployment matches or beats a context-blind OLS
benchmark fitted on all displayed rows
(Appendix Table~\ref{tab:coin-city-vs-ols}). Naming the opposite regime reverses
the assignment rather than degrading it. Both effects are transient: by $k=4$ the
cue advantage narrows to $0.12$--$0.23$ points, so target observations displace
the cue once they disagree.

\textbf{The cue selects a displayed reference, not a prior on its words.} Those
numbers could otherwise come from a prior about news coverage alone. Partialling
out the regime separates the accounts, because a fit to four noisy reference cases
carries sampling error no cue-determined prior can reproduce, and all four arms
then behave as reference selection predicts at $k=0$: with no reference cases
neither loads, with no cue both load equally, the correct cue concentrates loading
on the named reference ($.51$--$.86$) and strips it from the other
($.04$--$.10$), and inversion moves it onto the reference the false cue names
($.61$--$.90$). This also caps implied-slope correlations near $.66$ rather than
$.996$, since a transferred estimate inherits its source's noise
(Appendix~\ref{app:coin-city-reference-selection}).

\textbf{Induction of a novel mapping.} This mapping must be inferred from each
episode's own examples. Twelve of fifteen systems recover it at $k=0$, all five
hosted ones among them, and within both Qwen3 and Llama only the larger
checkpoints do, so the transition differs by family rather than tracking parameter
count. Hidden states carry the mapping weakly and only after computation:
semantic-arm probes reach AUC $1.000$ but read the first transformer block, where
input embeddings alone already reach $1.000$. Under arbitrary labels that control
falls to chance and the decoded regime follows the label at $k=0$ and the truth at
$k=4$ (Figure~\ref{fig:coin-city-symbol-decoding}). Across five checkpoints
decodability tracks behavior, and within Qwen3 it crosses into significance at the
same checkpoint the forecasts do ($.520$, $.566$, $.678$ from 4B to 14B). Patching those states shifts
Qwen3-14B's forecast by $.333$ at $k=0$ ($p<.0001$) and does nothing at $k=4$, so
the induced mapping causes the zero-shot forecast
(Appendix~\ref{app:coin-city-mechanistic-probe}).

Context therefore selects which demonstrated relationship a model applies, that
selection reaches a particular displayed reference rather than a remembered
association, and target evidence overrides it. Three things bound the reading: the
cue is binary and the regimes far apart, so selection is a two-way choice rather
than a graded one; the exact within-regime coefficient is never recovered, failing
in every model--$k$ cell; and the mechanistic evidence covers five open
checkpoints with the causal test on one, the hosted deployments being behavioral
throughout. Experiment~3 tests transfer after episode-matched training.
