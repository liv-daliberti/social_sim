\section{Forecasting, induction, and model validity}
\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{Judgmental forecasting.}
Strong human forecasters combine base rates, decomposition, diverse evidence,
and repeated revision \citep{tetlock2005expert,mellers2014psychological,tetlock2016superforecasting}.
Forecasting benchmarks increasingly evaluate LMs on time-indexed or
leakage-controlled questions \citep{zou2022forecasting,karger2025forecastbench};
retrieval and ensembling bring selected systems near crowd accuracy
\citep{halawi2024approaching,schoenegger2024wisdom}. These results show what
probabilities models produce, but not what drives them. Our focus is the
relationship behind the update:
whether evidence is treated as probative, which contextual analogue is selected,
and whether that choice survives a change in domain or mechanism.

\paragraph{Induction and behavioral evidence.}
Transformers infer unseen functions from input--output examples within their
training distribution \citep{garg2022what,chan2022data}; LMs also solve
selected abstract analogy tasks \citep{webb2023emergent,yasunaga2024large}. Our
selection tests ask a different question. The target displays no examples of
its own, so a relationship must be carried from a demonstrated analogue under a
qualitative cue, and an arbitrary-label variant reverses the cue's mapping across
episodes. World-model accounts emphasize representations of environmental state
or dynamics \citep{ha2018world,lecun2022path,li2023emergent}; shortcut-learning
results document failures under structural shift
\citep{geirhos2020shortcut,mitchell2023ai,vafa2025foundation}. We infer process
from predicted changes and invariances in paired behavioral tests, not from
accuracy alone.
