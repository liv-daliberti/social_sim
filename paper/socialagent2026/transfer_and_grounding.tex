\section{Can relationship use survive a change of world?}
\label{sec:transfer}

\input{../fig_exp3_transfer_grid}

Coin City establishes relationship selection within a prompt. The transfer
study tests whether training makes that operation portable. Improvements in
response format, answer marginals, or generic campaign--poll knowledge do not
require matching a particular episode's evidence to its outcome. The transfer
study isolates that match.

\subsection{Transfer under domain and mechanism shift}
\label{sec:exp3}

Training occurs only in Coin City under the direct, one-period relationship
\(x_t\rightarrow y_{t+1}\). Every prompt contains two complete reference cases, a
partially observed target, a pathway description, and a multi-outcome query. The
\emph{episode-matched} arm pairs prompt \(i\) with its own answer key. The
\emph{population-prior} control uses byte-identical prompts and the same multiset
of answer keys, but applies a fixed within-evidence-depth derangement. Thus both
arms see the same inputs and answer distribution; only the prompt--outcome
correspondence is removed from the control.

Evaluation crosses two changes. Coin Harbor replaces the campaign-poll surface
domain. A mediated, persistent mechanism replaces the direct response. Their
factorial combination yields in-distribution, domain-shift, mechanism-shift, and
joint-shift cells, none of which names a graph to the model. The primary
comparison is population-prior minus episode-matched response MAE in the joint
cell. Training uses three seeds per arm, and evaluation averages five stochastic
draws on 1,440 shared tasks before a paired seed-first bootstrap
(Appendix~\ref{app:exp3-complete-training-setup}).

\paragraph{Finding: episode matching adds a distinct transfer gain.}
For Qwen3-8B under joint shift, response MAE is \(5.72\) for the untrained base,
\(4.71\) after population-prior training, and \(4.46\) after episode-matched
training. The matched advantage over the distribution control is
\(.251\,[.058,.470]\). It has the lower point estimate in all four cells, although
the structure-only interval crosses zero. Qwen3-4B replicates the joint-cell
contrast. Removing or reversing the pathway description also degrades the matched
model. Transferred forecasts therefore remain responsive to relational language.

The control clarifies the size of the structural contribution. Episode matching
accounts for about one fifth of Qwen3-8B's fail-closed improvement over base in
the joint cell; shared format and population learning account for the rest.
Llama-3.1-8B does not reproduce the factorial pattern and has effects opposite
the hypothesis in two cells. Contextual relationship use transfers in the two tested
Qwen sizes; the tested Llama model provides no evidence of transfer. Family and
size are confounded here: only one Llama size was trained, and it is the bottom of
a family whose arbitrary-cue threshold Section~\ref{sec:exp2} places far above
Qwen's, at 70B against 7--14B. Whether transfer carries a similarly
family-dependent threshold is untested.

\subsection{External grounding: sealed historical markets}
\label{sec:exp4}

A separate study evaluates outcome-based training beyond the synthetic world. Resolved political, governmental, macroeconomic, and geopolitical markets
are censored before resolution. Prompts contain only the question, rules, cutoff,
and genuine pre-cutoff prices; outcomes and post-cutoff fields are withheld.
Calendar splits prevent event and normalized question families from crossing
training, development, and test. Qwen3 and Llama checkpoints receive rank-32 LoRA
training with reward \(1-(\hat p-y)^2\), the complement of Brier loss. A
318-market holdout is disjoint from training, development, and the earlier
1,024-market test, and every endpoint is evaluated with five stochastic draws
(Appendix~\ref{app:exp3b-protocol}).

Proper-score training lowers mean Brier relative to every tested base checkpoint.
This improvement on sealed events anchors the training gain outside generated
worlds. The experiment identifies neither monotonic scaling nor crowd
superiority. The trained Qwen series is
nonmonotonic from 1.7B to 14B, two Llama sizes do not identify a family curve,
and the 14B Qwen and 8B Llama intervals relative to contemporaneous Polymarket
both cross zero. Hosted systems are categorical reference points, not members of
the local scaling curve.

The controlled factorial separates episode-level relationship learning from
shared format and answer-distribution gains. The historical study measures
training effects on real, sealed events. Relationship use and external accuracy
are separate claims.
