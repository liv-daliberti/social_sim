\section{Can relationship use survive a change of world?}
\label{sec:transfer}

An improvement after finetuning is ambiguous: the model may have learned how
evidence produced the outcome, or only the usual format and answers. Section~3
tested relationship use within a prompt. Section~4 asks whether training carries
that relationship into a new world, then whether it helps on unseen real events.

\subsection{Exp.~3: transfer under domain and mechanism shift}
\label{sec:exp3}

\textbf{Why matching matters.}
A training gain could reflect the prompt format or answer distribution rather
than learning which evidence produces which outcome. The two arms therefore use
byte-identical prompts, the same target-answer multiset, and the same training
budget; only the case--answer pairing differs. Shuffled-pair training captures
generic task learning. Any matched-over-shuffled gain comes from preserving the
evidence--outcome link; survival under a new domain and response mechanism
supports reuse of that link beyond Coin City.

We test that survival by changing both the setting and the way effects unfold.
Coin City campaign news and candidate support become Coin Harbor shipping
bulletins and a cargo-flow index over tide cycles, with different populations,
noise, units, and time scale. A persistent two-step response replaces the
direct one-step response.
The $2\times2$ design in Figure~\ref{fig:exp3-transfer-grid} tests each change
separately and jointly; full details are in
App.~\ref{app:exp3-complete-training-setup}.

\noindent\textbf{Finding: episode matching adds a transfer gain.}
Figure~\ref{fig:exp3-transfer-results} shows the same qualitative result across
the transfer grid and tested Qwen sizes: preserving the case--answer pairing adds
value beyond exposure to the same prompts and answer distribution. The gain
narrows with scale, and reversing the pathway description hurts, so the evidence
supports reusable relationship use within the tested Qwen family rather than a
format-learning effect or scale invariance. Full cell estimates, intervals,
seed-level patterns, the cross-size synthesis, and the Llama comparisons are in
App.~\ref{app:exp3-coin-qwen-results}.

\noindent\textbf{\emph{What Exp.~3 establishes.}}
\emph{For the tested Qwen models, episode-matched training adds a transfer gain
beyond learning the task's format and answer distribution, including when both
the domain and response mechanism change.}

\subsection{Exp.~4: external grounding in unseen historical markets}
\label{sec:exp4}

Experiment~3 makes transfer legible by deliberately changing a known domain and
a known response mechanism. Historical markets do not come with those factors
labeled, and we cannot enumerate which mechanisms differ between training and
evaluation. Experiment~4 asks whether the same principle survives that loss of
control: train on information paired with its realized outcome, then forecast
family-disjoint events whose governing relationships are unknown. We train on
resolved markets using only information available before each outcome, then
evaluate 318 unseen questions from different event families. Error is the squared
distance between the forecast probability and what happened, so lower Brier
scores are better (App.~\ref{app:exp3b-protocol}).

Figure~\ref{fig:sealed-market-grounding} first compares each trained model with its
base, then tests whether the gain persists as the base becomes stronger. Training lowers
error at every tested size, but Qwen performance does not improve steadily and
the gap shrinks from 1.7B to 32B. At 32B, trained mean Brier is \(.1265\)
versus \(.1266\) for the base, the smallest observed gain; the trained-minus-base
interval \([-.00045,.00029]\) includes zero. A Llama 3B/8B
replication shows the same qualitative convergence pattern
(App. Figure~\ref{fig:exp4-llama-scale-results}). Several trained checkpoints
reach contemporaneous Polymarket performance. Hosted systems provide context,
not additional points on the local scaling curve.

\noindent\textbf{\emph{What Exp.~4 establishes.}}
\emph{The central idea from the synthetic study extends to naturally
heterogeneous forecasting: pairing evidence with its outcome lowers mean error
on held-out event families even when domains and causal mechanisms are neither
labeled nor deliberately swapped. This makes the Experiment~3 result a candidate
general training principle rather than a Coin City-specific effect, although its
incremental benefit narrows as the Qwen base strengthens and is uncertain at
32B.}

Experiments~3 and 4 answer complementary parts of the same claim. The synthetic
study identifies why matching evidence to outcomes matters under controlled
shifts; the historical study asks whether that principle has predictive value
when the world supplies the shifts and their structure is unknown. Together they
connect relationship use to external accuracy.
