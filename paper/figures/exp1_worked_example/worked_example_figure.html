The model

The event model GPT-5.4 builds — what actually decides this question

Causal mechanisms
  • Presidential action can directly create or direct a federal AI-release review framework.
  • To qualify it must review the public release of new models — not procurement or internal use.
  • The June 2, 2026 executive order already set up a voluntary pre-release access framework.
  • Resolution turns on whether that voluntary order counts — or whether follow-up action makes it binding.
◆ Pivotal latent variable
Does the voluntary June-2 framework count as a qualifying federal “review” — or is a binding new action still needed before June 30?

Why 0.46. Trump had already signed a June-2 executive order creating a voluntary pre-release federal-access and benchmarking framework for frontier models — a big jump from the 22% prior. But the same White House fact sheet explicitly disclaims mandatory licensing or pre-clearance, so it settles just below 50%: it is genuinely unclear whether resolvers will count a voluntary framework as a “review process.”

The evidence

An evidence packet is injected — web search off; the agent can only reason from it

↳ pro-H₁ · targets the pivotal latent · plausibility 4/5
WASHINGTON · June 10, 2026 synthetic evidence packet
OMB tracking figures show agencies racing to finalize AI-release review framework

Internal Office of Management and Budget tracking figures reviewed by Reuters showed 14 federal agencies had returned comments this week on a draft framework for reviewing the public release of certain advanced AI models — with target deadlines of June 24 for final criteria and June 27 for presidential sign-off. Commerce, Defense, Homeland Security and Justice were listed with implementation tasks. The compressed timeline and cross-agency staffing suggest the administration is preparing a binding federal process before the end of June.

targets → Whether a binding, White-House-directed review of public AI-model releases is already being drafted and coordinated — the voluntary-vs-binding crux the agent flagged as pivotal.

Updated rationale. This doesn't just reinterpret the June-2 order — it points to a near-complete interagency process for a draft framework explicitly reviewing the public release of advanced AI models, with concrete deadlines before June 30 and a planned presidential sign-off. That sharply raises the odds of a qualifying action; the residual doubt is only whether the final version is framed as binding enough for resolvers to count.

reading
Two near-mirror packets swing the forecast +21 and −19 points across the 50% line; the AI-adjacent-but-irrelevant one holds at −2. The update tracks the variable the agent said mattered — will a qualifying federal review actually land — not the mere presence of AI-policy news. That is the signature of a coherent, updateable event model.
Figure X. A single Experiment-1 episode illustrating direction-selective belief updating. GPT-5.4 forecasts whether Trump orders a federal review of AI-model releases by June 30, 2026 (initial p̂ = 0.46 = P(H₁), above the 0.38 Polymarket price). It isolates one pivotal latent variable — whether the existing voluntary June-2 executive order counts as a qualifying federal “review,” or a binding new action is still needed. A confirming packet (an interagency draft framework with a June-27 sign-off) raises the forecast to 0.67 (+21 pp); a mirror-image disconfirming packet (a White House statement ruling out any June order) lowers it to 0.27 (−19 pp); an orthogonal AI-adjacent packet (data-center power strain) leaves it essentially unchanged (0.44, −2 pp). Hypothesis posterior and headline forecast move together throughout (p̂ = P(H₁); ICS preserved). Web search is disabled during updates, so revisions are driven only by the injected evidence.
